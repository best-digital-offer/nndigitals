How to Find Background and Lazy-Loaded Images on a Webpage

By N&N Digitals · webpage image extractor, save website images

A webpage can contain more images than are immediately visible. Some appear as standard image elements, others are set as CSS backgrounds, and some are loaded only when a visitor scrolls. This is why a basic right-click workflow may miss assets that a dedicated webpage image extractor can help surface.

Build a practical workflow

Standard images are usually easy to identify, but background images are often part of a page's styling. They may be used for banners, cards or decorative sections. Lazy-loaded images are different: the website waits until the user approaches an image before downloading it. Scrolling through the page can help trigger those requests.

Keep your information organized

A useful extraction workflow should provide a preview so you can distinguish a full-size image from a thumbnail or placeholder. Dimensions and file type are helpful clues. A large image may be suitable for a design reference, while a tiny file may be an icon or a low-resolution preview.

Important considerations

Modern websites may serve responsive image variants. The same visual asset can have several URLs or sizes depending on screen and layout. A tool may list more than one version. Choose the version that meets your needs and avoid downloading every duplicate by default.

Choose the right approach

Use extracted images responsibly. Some may be licensed, copyrighted, private or part of a restricted service. Do not assume that an image can be republished simply because it can be detected by your browser. When in doubt, contact the rights holder or use properly licensed alternatives.
