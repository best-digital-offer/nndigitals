How to Screenshot Multiple Tabs in Chrome for Research and Work

By N&N Digitals · screenshot multiple tabs

Research sessions often involve several pages at once: product comparisons, help documentation, competitor pages, articles and dashboards. Capturing each tab individually can interrupt your work. A multiple-tab screenshot workflow helps collect the relevant pages in a consistent way and keeps the visual references together.

Build a practical workflow

Start by grouping related tabs in Chrome. Decide whether you need every open tab or only a few specific pages. TabSnap lets you select individual tabs or capture all open tabs, so you can avoid saving unrelated pages. Review the tab list before starting, especially if your browser also contains personal or confidential pages.

Keep your information organized

This workflow is useful for competitive research, website audits, design reviews, support documentation and content planning. A researcher can preserve several source pages, a QA tester can document multiple states, and a project manager can assemble visual references for a review. Keep filenames clear so you can identify each capture later.

Important considerations

For long webpages, full-page capture is more informative than a screenshot of only the visible viewport. It can preserve the page's overall structure and reduce the need to take repeated screenshots while scrolling. Check each capture for missing sections, loading placeholders or overlays before using it in a report.

Choose the right approach

PNG works well for individual visual references, while PDF can be convenient when you want a shareable document. Keep the original source URLs in your notes if you may need to revisit the live pages. A screenshot records appearance at a moment in time and may become outdated as the site changes.
